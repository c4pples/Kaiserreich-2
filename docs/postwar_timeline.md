# Postwar Timeline

This is the default timeline from the closing phase of the Second Weltkrieg to the opening crisis of the Third Weltkrieg. Dates can be adjusted for gameplay pacing, but the causal chain should remain intact.

## 1939-1941: The Second Weltkrieg

- The Third Internationale and Germany enter open war after years of border crises, rearmament, propaganda conflict, and economic rivalry.
- The Entente opens secondary fronts when it judges that the syndicalist powers are committed and vulnerable.
- Russia exploits the war to challenge the eastern settlement, though its intervention does not produce a clean victory.
- The war quickly becomes continental and imperial: shipping, colonies, oil, and client states matter as much as the Rhine.

## 1942: The Long Crisis Year

- Germany survives the first shock but pays for survival with emergency controls, debt, and dependence on allies.
- France becomes the decisive western battlefield.
- Britain faces blockade, air war, internal pressure, and the return of loyalist networks.
- Eastern Europe is militarized into a defensive belt, but loyalty varies sharply by country.
- Japan expands its leverage in Asia while European powers are distracted.

## 1943: Defeat of the Internationale

- Paris falls after a costly campaign.
- The Commune government fragments between evacuation, last-stand militancy, and underground resistance.
- The Union of Britain collapses after invasion and internal fracture.
- Socialist Italy is defeated, but no Italian settlement satisfies all claimants.
- The Entente returns to London and Paris, but it returns through coalition warfare rather than autonomous triumph.

## 1944: The Peace That Does Not Settle

- A European settlement is imposed through overlapping treaties rather than one clean conference.
- Germany secures recognition of its eastern order and a demilitarized western security system.
- Restored France resents restrictions on its army, colonies, and diplomacy.
- Britain regains formal sovereignty while accepting bases, debts, and reconstruction conditions.
- Anti-syndicalist tribunals begin; syndicalist undergrounds reorganize.
- Russia refuses to accept the legitimacy of the settlement.

## 1945-1946: Reconstruction and Retrenchment

- Europe demobilizes incompletely. Veterans, refugees, and purged administrators reshape domestic politics.
- Mitteleuropa is expanded into a reconstruction mechanism, giving Germany leverage over trade, credit, and industrial allocation.
- The Entente debates whether it remains a liberation alliance or becomes a postwar security bloc.
- National France's return to the metropole leaves North Africa politically unstable.
- Japan consolidates clients and economic concessions in Asia.
- The former United States settles into exhausted armistice lines, with rival claimants beginning reconstruction while foreign powers compete for recognition, access, and influence.

## 1947: The Frozen Peace

- German intelligence identifies surviving syndicalist networks across France, Britain, Italy, Iberia, and the Low Countries.
- Russian military modernization accelerates under the language of national security and anti-hegemonic liberation.
- France quietly seeks room to rebuild beyond German limits.
- Britain rebuilds naval and intelligence capacity but cannot fund a full imperial restoration.
- Colonial anti-imperial movements learn from syndicalist organization even when rejecting syndicalist ideology.

## 1948: Peripheral Wars

- Proxy conflicts erupt in the Balkans, the Middle East, Central Asia, and Southeast Asia.
- Germany and Russia compete through arms, advisers, rail concessions, and border guarantees.
- Japan backs anti-colonial and anti-European forces when useful, while suppressing movements inside its own sphere.
- Entente governments discover that restoration does not automatically restore loyalty.
- Former syndicalist officers and organizers become valuable to insurgents, exiles, and state intelligence services.

## 1949: The Alliance Crisis

- The Reichspakt hardens into a security bloc with integrated planning.
- The Entente is divided between cooperation with Germany and independent great-power recovery.
- Russia formalizes a counter-bloc around anti-German revisionism.
- Japan and Russia begin selective coordination despite mutual suspicion.
- The American successor states face pressure to define their positions before another European war reshapes the world without a unified American voice.

## 1950: Opening of the Third Weltkrieg Era

- A crisis in Eastern Europe, the Black Sea, or the Near East triggers mobilization.
- Germany frames the crisis as defense of the legal postwar order.
- Russia frames it as liberation from German encirclement.
- France and Britain must decide whether the German order protects them or imprisons them.
- Japan tests the Pacific balance while Europe mobilizes.
- The former United States becomes the swing continental system whose fragmentation shapes the global war.

## Timeline Design Notes

- The Second Weltkrieg should feel won at enormous cost, not cleanly solved.
- No major power should enter the Third Weltkrieg without a domestic reason for doing so.
- Regional crises should be playable as limited wars, escalation chains, or delayed flashpoints.
- Every start-date conflict should trace back to a postwar compromise that failed.
