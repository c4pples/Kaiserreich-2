# Revived Nationalism

Revived nationalism is the central political trigger of the Third Weltkrieg era.

The Second Weltkrieg discredits syndicalism as the main open challenger to the German world order. The Commune of France, Union of Britain, and Socialist Italy have fallen. The Internationale no longer stands as a normal great-power bloc. But the defeat of syndicalism does not create peace. It creates a vacuum that nationalist, anti-colonial, restorationist, and revanchist forces fill.

## Core Thesis

The world turns away from syndicalism as the primary revolutionary language and toward national revival.

This does not mean the world becomes less radical. It means radicalism becomes more national:

- client states demand sovereignty from their protectors,
- colonies reject restored imperial rule,
- defeated societies seek revenge through national rebirth,
- veterans and officers become political organizers,
- great powers sponsor nationalism abroad while suppressing it at home,
- and syndicalist remnants survive by embedding themselves inside national liberation struggles.

## Why It Happens

### Germany Wins Again

Germany's victory proves that the Reichspakt order can survive, but also that it survives by discipline, dependence, and supervision. Eastern European allies and clients increasingly ask whether German protection has become permanent tutelage.

### The Entente Returns

The restored British and French states return claiming legitimacy, but their empires do not automatically accept restoration. Colonized peoples hear liberation rhetoric in Europe and apply it to themselves.

### Syndicalism Is Defeated

The defeat of the Internationale damages the prestige of international syndicalist revolution. Some militants remain syndicalist or totalist, but many local movements shift toward national liberation, anti-occupation politics, and revenge.

### Russia Reframes Revision

Russia presents itself as the patron of national recovery against German artificial borders. It can support nationalists even when their ideology differs from Moscow's domestic system.

### Japan Weaponizes Anti-Colonialism

Japan encourages anti-European nationalism in Asia and the Pacific, but fears nationalist movements inside its own sphere. This contradiction should be a major source of crisis.

## National Populism in Kaiserreich 2

National Populism should be used more often than in the original 1936 setup, especially in puppets, mandates, colonies, and defeated or humiliated states.

Use National Populism for movements that combine:

- national humiliation,
- mass mobilization,
- charismatic or militarized leadership,
- anti-elite or anti-foreign rhetoric,
- paramilitary culture,
- ethnic, religious, or civilizational language,
- and a promise of national rebirth through unity and force.

Do not use it for every nationalist movement. Some nationalists should remain social conservative, paternal autocrat, authoritarian democratic, social democratic, or even socialist. National Populism is the radical mass form of revived nationalism, not the entire category.

## Where It Should Appear

### German Puppets and Reichspakt Clients

National Populist currents should be common in states that feel protected but humiliated:

- Poland,
- Ukraine,
- White Ruthenia/Belarus,
- the Baltic states,
- parts of the Balkans,
- possible Caucasus buffers,
- and colonial or mandate administrations tied to German security.

These movements may still hate Russia. Their conflict with Germany is about sovereignty, army control, tariffs, language, land, and dignity.

### Entente Colonies and Mandates

National Populist and anti-colonial nationalist movements should challenge restored British and French authority:

- North Africa,
- West Africa,
- Egypt and the Canal zone,
- India,
- Southeast Asian possessions,
- Caribbean and Atlantic dependencies.

The tone should vary by region. Some movements are secular modernizers, some clerical, some military, some ethnic-national, and some social revolutionary.

### Japanese Sphere

Japan sponsors Asian nationalism against Europe, but client nationalists may turn against Japanese control. National Populism here can be both anti-European and anti-Japanese depending on the stage of the campaign.

### Former Syndicalist States

France, Britain, and Italy should retain syndicalist undergrounds, but National Populism should grow among those who interpret defeat as national shame:

- anti-syndicalist veterans,
- restored-state hardliners,
- collaborators seeking redemption,
- former workers who now prioritize national recovery,
- and paramilitary leagues.

### Fragmented America

American successor states are fertile ground for revived nationalism:

- the American Union/National State as a major National Populist candidate,
- Federal emergency nationalists who demand reunification,
- Pacific militarists focused on survival and the Pacific threat,
- border militias and interior authorities built around local sovereignty.

## Relationship to Syndicalism

Syndicalism is still present, but it is no longer the main open world pole by default.

Its new roles:

- underground resistance,
- labor unrest,
- exile networks,
- ideological memory,
- anti-colonial organizer,
- occasional ruling ideology in isolated survivor states,
- and possible comeback path if the player rebuilds it.

Totalism becomes the revolutionary authoritarian lesson of defeat. Anarchism becomes the anti-state lesson of defeat. National Populism becomes the nationalist lesson of defeat, humiliation, and dependency.

## Gameplay Systems

Recommended systems:

- national revival meter for client states,
- colonial sovereignty pressure,
- bloc cohesion penalties from nationalist governments,
- foreign-sponsored nationalist parties,
- paramilitary integration decisions,
- mandate legitimacy,
- autonomy versus security tradeoffs,
- anti-syndicalist laws that accidentally empower nationalist hardliners.

## Outbreak Role

Revived nationalism should not remain background flavor. It should be the mechanism that breaks the postwar order.

The most plausible Third Weltkrieg opening is a synchronized but not perfectly coordinated revolt across German-aligned clients and treaty states. These movements do not all share ideology or foreign patrons. Some are National Populist, some military-nationalist, some conservative-sovereigntist, some social democratic, and some influenced by syndicalist labor networks. What unites them is rejection of permanent German supervision.

The crisis becomes global because outside powers treat those revolts as a test of the settlement:

- Russia backs anti-German nationalist governments.
- France and Britain split between fear of Germany and fear of renewed continental chaos.
- Japan uses the crisis to attack European positions in Asia.
- American successor states use recognition, loans, volunteers, or embargoes to gain leverage.
- German loyalists in threatened states ask Berlin for direct protection.

## Writing Rules

- Write nationalist movements as locally rooted, not interchangeable.
- Give each movement a concrete grievance: taxes, officers, language, land, bases, borders, tariffs, forced demobilization, colonial law, or humiliation.
- Avoid making National Populism a universal villain tag with identical dialogue everywhere.
- Show great powers trying to use nationalism and then discovering it has its own agenda.
- Keep syndicalism alive as a shadow, but make national revival the sound everyone hears in the streets.
