# Global Balance of Power

The Third Weltkrieg era should be built around unstable multipolarity. The Second Weltkrieg decided who survived, not who could rule the world.

## Core Strategic Triangle

### Germany

Strengths:

- Best-positioned European industrial system.
- Reichspakt command structure and Mitteleuropa logistics.
- Prestige from surviving two Weltkriege.
- Strong military planning culture.

Weaknesses:

- Debt, veterans, reconstruction strain, and imperial overstretch.
- Allies resent German supervision.
- Naval limitations compared with combined Entente or Japanese pressure.
- Vulnerable to simultaneous Russian crisis, colonial unrest, and western diplomatic defection.

Gameplay role:

- Defensive hegemon trying to preserve an order it can no longer cheaply afford.

### Russia

Strengths:

- Strategic depth, manpower, and revisionist energy.
- Ability to threaten the entire eastern settlement.
- Diplomatic appeal to states dissatisfied with German dominance.
- Flexibility to support insurgencies and nationalist movements.

Weaknesses:

- Modernization gaps, infrastructure strain, and internal political coercion.
- Suspicion from neighbors with memories of Russian rule.
- Potential overextension across Europe, Caucasus, Central Asia, and the Far East.
- Uneasy relationship with Japan.

Gameplay role:

- Revisionist land power that can force escalation even before it is fully ready.

### Japan

Strengths:

- Dominant regional navy and forward position in East Asia.
- Access to client economies and resource zones.
- Ability to exploit European distraction.
- Anti-European rhetoric useful in colonial theaters.

Weaknesses:

- Resource vulnerability if sea lanes are contested.
- Client instability and resistance.
- Potential confrontation with the United States, Germany, Russia, and Entente remnants.
- Ideological contradiction between liberation rhetoric and imperial practice.

Gameplay role:

- Pacific opportunist whose actions can globalize a European crisis.

## Secondary Great Powers

### United Kingdom

Britain is restored but diminished. Its value lies in naval bases, intelligence, finance, and symbolic legitimacy. It should be able to rebuild into a major actor, but only through hard choices about empire, welfare settlement, and alignment.

### French Republic

France is the most politically volatile restored power. It wants revenge, security, and recognition. Its army and colonial empire are central gameplay questions: rebuild under German limits, break constraints, reconcile society, or descend into reaction.

### United States

The former United States is the swing continental system, not a unified superpower at game start. Its successor states collectively possess immense industry, manpower, ports, and resources, but no single American government can automatically project all of that power abroad. The mod should support possible reunification, negotiated confederation, or permanent division without rewriting every global file. Use scripted global variables or start-game rules to define:

- dominant American claimant,
- degree of fragmentation,
- status of federal institutions,
- foreign policy doctrine,
- Pacific readiness,
- and attitude toward European blocs.

### Austria-Hungary or Successor Danubian Order

The Danubian space should be a balancing region rather than a footnote. If Austria-Hungary survives, it is a conservative stabilizer wary of German domination. If it reforms or fragments, its successors become central to Balkan and Central European crisis chains.

## Regional Pressure Points

## Main War Trigger

The Third Weltkrieg should be triggered less by a revived Internationale and more by revived nationalism. Syndicalism and totalism remain dangerous, but the main global engine is the revolt of nations, colonies, clients, and defeated societies against permanent tutelage.

The German victory creates the conditions:

- Reichspakt clients want German protection but reject German management.
- Restored Entente empires want old legitimacy back, while colonial subjects demand national sovereignty.
- Russia turns national recovery into a doctrine of continental revision.
- Japan encourages anti-European nationalism while trying to prevent the same force from escaping its own sphere.
- American successor states build rival national myths around reunification.
- Former syndicalist regions reframe defeat as national humiliation as much as class defeat.

Gameplay use:

- nationalist coups in client states,
- anti-colonial crises in mandates,
- army factions demanding independent war planning,
- diplomatic incidents over bases, railways, tariffs, and inspectors,
- foreign powers funding nationalist movements in rival spheres,
- bloc cohesion loss when a protected state refuses orders.

Default outbreak model:

- The Postwar Reckoning weakens German credit and client-state legitimacy.
- Germany demands emergency economic and military compliance across the Reichspakt.
- Several clients refuse, revolt, or split between loyalist and nationalist governments.
- Russia recognizes or arms anti-German governments.
- France and Britain debate whether to support Germany, mediate, or back anti-German revision.
- Japan exploits the distraction in Asia and the Pacific.
- American successor states choose whether to stay inward, seek recognition, or intervene through loans and volunteers.
- Some states side with Germany out of fear of Russia, domestic revolution, or territorial rivals.

## Systemic Crisis Trigger

The revived-nationalism era needs an economic and institutional shock equivalent in gameplay importance to Kaiserreich's Black Monday. The default name is the Postwar Reckoning.

The Postwar Reckoning is not simply a stock-market crash. It is a cascading crisis of war debt, reconstruction credit, colonial extraction, demobilization, food and fuel prices, veteran benefits, currency confidence, and client-state dependency. It exposes that Germany preserved its system by making nearly every ally, restored state, colony, and mandate dependent on financial arrangements that cannot all be honored at once.

Gameplay purpose:

- weaken the legitimacy of moderate governments,
- create openings for anarchists, syndicalists, totalists, and National Populists,
- strain Reichspakt and Entente cohesion,
- force majors to choose between austerity, repression, reform, or externalization,
- and produce the social atmosphere in which nationalist crises become war triggers.

### Eastern Europe

Primary fault line between Germany and Russia. The key questions are sovereignty, guarantees, minorities, and military basing.

Design use:

- border crises,
- coup attempts,
- rail and mobilization decisions,
- intelligence wars,
- alliance defection mechanics.

### The Balkans

Too fractured for simple bloc control. Every great power can intervene, but local rivalries should remain primary.

Design use:

- limited wars that may trigger guarantee chains,
- arms races,
- dynastic and republican legitimacy disputes,
- Danubian versus Russian versus German influence.

### Middle East

Post-Ottoman or Ottoman-survival outcomes should be modular. Oil, canals, pilgrimage routes, minorities, and great-power concessions make the region a natural escalation arena.

Design use:

- energy security,
- protectorate negotiations,
- nationalist revolts,
- competing military missions.

### Africa

European restoration collides with anti-colonial mobilization and the legacy of Mittelafrika.

Design use:

- colonial overstretch,
- liberation movements,
- proxy arms routes,
- legitimacy costs for restored empires.

### East Asia and the Pacific

The core Japan-versus-countercoalition theater. China's condition, American recovery, German concessions, and Entente bases determine the shape of war.

Design use:

- naval escalation,
- resource embargoes,
- Chinese unification or fragmentation paths,
- island-chain defense and basing decisions.

### Latin America

Not every crisis needs to start in Europe. Latin America offers ideological rivalry, American doctrine tests, trade warfare, and naval access disputes.

Design use:

- competing development missions,
- anti-colonial diplomacy,
- canal and Caribbean security,
- limited conflicts that influence American alignment.

## System Recommendations

- Use global tension in regional bands rather than one universal escalation meter.
- Track bloc cohesion separately from faction membership.
- Give major powers domestic fatigue variables that constrain foreign action.
- Let middle powers exploit crises for concessions before joining wars.
- Make ideological subversion slower than military alignment.
- Treat colonial control as a costly system, not free manpower and resources.

## Balance Principle

No bloc should be able to win the Third Weltkrieg by pressing one front. Victory should require solving at least three problems:

- domestic legitimacy,
- alliance cohesion,
- and control of a secondary theater.
