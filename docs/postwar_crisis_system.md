# Postwar Crisis System

The Kaiserreich 2 equivalent to Black Monday should be a major global shock that creates the political environment for anarchism, syndicalism, totalism, and National Populism to thrive.

Working name: the Postwar Reckoning.

This should feel structurally similar to Kaiserreich's Black Monday in gameplay importance: a sudden crisis that every important country must answer through focuses, events, decisions, and national spirits. It should not be a simple repeat of the original crash. It belongs to the post-Second Weltkrieg world.

## Core Concept

The Postwar Reckoning is the moment the victorious postwar order discovers that it cannot pay for itself.

Germany won by preserving its system, but that system now rests on:

- war debt,
- reconstruction loans,
- emergency military spending,
- veterans' benefits,
- colonial resource extraction,
- client-state trade dependency,
- Entente restoration costs,
- demobilization unemployment,
- food and fuel shortages,
- unstable currencies,
- and fragile American credit due to continued fragmentation.

When confidence breaks, the crisis spreads through Mitteleuropa, Entente finance, colonial markets, Japanese resource networks, and American successor economies.

## Possible In-Universe Names

Use one primary name, but let countries describe it differently in localisation.

- The Postwar Reckoning
- The Credit Winter
- The Reconstruction Panic
- The Treaty Crash
- The Second Black Monday
- The Great Settlement Crisis

Recommendation: use `The Postwar Reckoning` as the neutral system name. Let journalists and radicals call it the Second Black Monday in events.

## Trigger Logic

The crisis should fire early, probably within the first 3-6 months of the start date.

Possible trigger chain:

1. A major German reconstruction bank fails or freezes credit.
2. Mitteleuropa clearing payments are suspended.
3. Restored France and Britain demand debt renegotiation.
4. Eastern European clients face currency runs and food-price spikes.
5. Colonial administrations increase extraction to cover deficits.
6. Veterans' leagues, unions, and nationalist parties mobilize.
7. Russia and Japan exploit the crisis through loans, propaganda, and arms routes.

## Who Is Hit

### Germany

Germany is hit through debt, overextended guarantees, export contraction, and loss of confidence in Mitteleuropa. The player must decide whether to preserve the system through austerity, reform, authoritarian controls, or external confrontation.

Radicals empowered:

- security technocrats,
- nationalist hardliners,
- anti-parliamentary conservatives,
- client-state separatists.

### Reichspakt Clients

Clients are hit by currency dependency, trade collapse, food shortages, and resentment of German inspectors.

Radicals empowered:

- National Populists,
- agrarian nationalists,
- anti-German military factions,
- syndicalist labor undergrounds in industrial regions.

### Restored France and Britain

Restored governments face the humiliation of victory without prosperity. Veterans, workers, colonial subjects, and old regime elites all demand incompatible solutions.

Radicals empowered:

- anti-syndicalist National Populists,
- syndicalist undergrounds,
- anarchist urban networks,
- imperial reactionaries,
- social democratic reconstructionists.

### Colonies and Mandates

Colonies experience the crisis as intensified extraction. Taxes rise, wages fall, food is requisitioned, and colonial governments demand loyalty while offering little.

Radicals empowered:

- anti-colonial National Populists,
- anarchist guerrilla networks,
- syndicalist labor organizers,
- religious-nationalist leagues,
- military independence movements.

### Fragmented America

The American successor states suffer from currency fragmentation, inter-state tariffs, disrupted rail networks, and recognition disputes. The crisis can push claimants toward reunification, war, or permanent separation.

Radicals empowered:

- American National Populists,
- syndicalist hardliners,
- anarchist local-defense networks,
- military emergency governments.

### Russia

Russia is affected but can exploit the crisis. It sells the idea that the German order is not only unjust but financially rotten.

Radicals empowered:

- National Maximalists,
- Eurasian Statists,
- Revolutionary Patriots,
- anti-German foreign-policy hawks.

### Japan

Japan gains opportunities but also faces resource shocks and client unrest. It may use the crisis to push deeper into Asia, while nationalist clients demand more autonomy.

Radicals empowered:

- militarists,
- pan-Asian nationalists,
- anti-Japanese client nationalists,
- anti-colonial partners abroad.

## Ideological Effects

The same economic crisis should feed different ideologies in different environments.

### Anarchism

Grows where people experience the state as absent or predatory.

Examples:

- food committees replacing failed administrations,
- local militias guarding neighborhoods,
- dockworker and rail communes,
- anti-tax rural self-defense,
- colonial guerrilla zones.

### Syndicalism

Grows where industrial labor blames employers, reconstruction boards, and anti-union laws.

Examples:

- illegal strikes,
- factory committees,
- railway sabotage,
- exile organizers returning under false names,
- labor parties splitting from legal social democrats.

### Totalism

Grows where revolutionary movements conclude that loose federations failed.

Examples:

- disciplined cadre parties,
- clandestine security networks,
- military-style labor fronts,
- revolutionary intelligence services,
- exile governments promising order after victory.

### National Populism

Grows where economic pain becomes a story of humiliation and betrayal.

Examples:

- German puppets blaming foreign inspectors,
- colonies blaming restored empires,
- veterans blaming weak parliaments,
- American claimants blaming rival successor states,
- Russian radicals blaming the German settlement.

## Design Rule

The Postwar Reckoning should not have one correct answer. Liberal reform, authoritarian stabilization, socialist compromise, nationalist mobilization, colonial extraction, and foreign borrowing should all work in some contexts while creating future costs.

The point is not only to hurt economies. The point is to make the player choose which radicals are allowed to grow, which institutions are sacrificed, and which foreign enemies are blamed.
