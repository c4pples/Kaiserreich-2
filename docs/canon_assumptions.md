# Canon Assumptions

This document defines the default post-Second Weltkrieg canon for Kaiserreich 2. It is a design baseline, not a hard ban on alternate starts. Variants should override these assumptions explicitly.

## Foundational Kaiserreich Canon

- Germany won the Weltkrieg and constructed the Reichspakt and Mitteleuropa to preserve its European hegemony.
- Britain, France, and parts of Italy were transformed by socialist revolution, producing the Third Internationale as the principal revolutionary bloc.
- The old Entente survived in exile through Canada, National France, and loyalist imperial remnants, bound by the goal of returning to the home islands and the metropole.
- Russia re-emerged as a revisionist great power seeking recovery of status, territory, and influence lost after the Weltkrieg and civil war.
- Japan exploited European distraction to pursue its own imperial sphere in East Asia and the Pacific.

## Default Second Weltkrieg Outcome

Kaiserreich 2 assumes a conditional Reichspakt-Entente victory in the Second Weltkrieg, followed by a failed peace.

This outcome is chosen because it preserves the widest number of recognizable KR actors while creating strong causes for a Third Weltkrieg:

- The Third Internationale is defeated militarily in Western Europe.
- The Union of Britain collapses after invasion, blockade, internal exhaustion, and royalist restoration pressure.
- The Commune of France is overrun after a prolonged continental campaign.
- Socialist Italy is defeated, but Italian unification remains politically contested.
- Germany survives the war but is financially strained, militarily exhausted, and politically less secure than its victory suggests.
- The Entente regains prestige but does not regain the uncontested imperial world it imagined.
- Russia avoids total defeat and remains the largest unresolved continental challenger.
- Japan retains enough strength to remain a Pacific rival, even if checked by German, American, or Entente pressure.

## What Germany Won

Germany won the Second Weltkrieg in the narrow but decisive sense that it prevented the destruction of the Kaiserreich system.

German victory means:

- the German Empire survives as the leading European power,
- the Reichspakt is preserved and hardened into a permanent security architecture,
- Mitteleuropa survives and expands into the main reconstruction mechanism,
- the Commune of France, Union of Britain, and Socialist Italy are destroyed as ruling states,
- the Entente returns to London and Paris through German-tolerated coalition victory,
- Germany's eastern order is internationally recognized again,
- and Russia fails to overturn the post-Weltkrieg settlement.

Germany did not win a clean imperial expansion.

Germany does not annex France, Britain, or the whole eastern frontier. It does not permanently solve Russia. It does not become unchallengeable at sea. It does not restore colonial stability. It does not turn the Entente into obedient clients. It wins security guarantees, treaty rights, economic leverage, demilitarized zones, and recognition of its order, but those gains come with debt, veteran politics, anti-German resentment, and a much larger security burden.

In short: Germany wins the war by maintaining control of its system and eliminating the immediate syndicalist threat. It loses the peace by proving that its system can only survive through permanent mobilization.

## Political Settlement

The postwar settlement is intentionally unsatisfying.

- Germany insists on a continental security order that protects Mitteleuropa and punishes revolutionary regimes.
- The restored French and British governments demand sovereignty, colonial recovery, and revenge for exile.
- Smaller states seek guarantees against both German domination and renewed socialism.
- Anti-syndicalist repression radicalizes surviving underground networks.
- Russia presents itself as the patron of nations threatened by German hegemony.
- Japan frames the European victory as proof that Asia must not remain subordinate to Atlantic empires.

## Major Continuity Decisions

### Germany

Germany remains a constitutional empire and the leading European power, but the victory is brittle. The Reichspakt is no longer merely a wartime alliance; it has become the central instrument of a German-led order. Berlin must balance security, debt, veteran politics, colonial instability, and resentment from states that were liberated but not treated as equals.

### France

The French Republic is restored in Paris, but the return from Algiers does not heal France. The state must integrate collaborators, exiles, resistance networks, veterans, and former syndicalist citizens. Its politics are defined by anti-syndicalist restoration, resentment toward Germany, and arguments over whether France has returned as a great power or as a German-protected client.

### Britain

The United Kingdom is restored, but Britain is poorer, divided, and dependent on imperial and Entente support. The monarchy returns with symbolic force, yet the post-revolutionary social settlement cannot simply be erased. Britain becomes a naval, intelligence, and financial actor seeking relevance without the industrial confidence it once possessed.

### Russia

Russia is neither destroyed nor satisfied. The default assumes Russia entered or supported the Second Weltkrieg but failed to break the German order decisively. Its government may vary by path, but its strategic role is constant: the great revisionist land power opposing the European settlement. Germany's second victory discredits moderate Russian accommodationists and strengthens ideologies promising national recovery, social mobilization, and revenge against the German order.

### United States

The United States remains fragmented by default. The Second American Civil War did not produce a clean national reunification before the Third Weltkrieg era. Instead, the former United States exists as a contested continental system of successor governments, demilitarized borders, provisional authorities, and foreign-backed security arrangements. This prevents America from functioning as an untouched liberal hegemon and makes the Western Hemisphere a major theater of diplomacy, reconstruction, espionage, and eventual escalation.

### Japan

Japan remains the central Asian and Pacific great power. It has not achieved uncontested mastery, but it has built enough economic and military depth to challenge European colonial restoration and American re-entry.

### Syndicalism, Totalism, and Anarchism

The defeat of the Third Internationale destroys the main syndicalist states in Western Europe, not the revolutionary tradition itself. Syndicalism survives through underground unions, exiles, colonial organizers, clandestine presses, and veterans' networks. Totalism survives as a harsher lesson drawn from defeat: the belief that the revolution failed because it was too decentralized, too parliamentary, or too merciful. Anarchism should be split from syndicalism as its own ideology, strongest among anti-state militias, federal communes, exile intellectual circles, and revolutionary movements that reject both restored reaction and totalist party discipline.

### Radicalization After German Victory

Germany's second victory should make the world more radical, not more stable. Anti-German states, defeated revolutionaries, humiliated nationalists, colonial movements, and even German hardliners all draw more extreme conclusions from the postwar settlement. The ideological map should therefore include intensified right-wing authoritarianism, revolutionary revanchism, anarchist anti-state currents, technocratic security states, and Russian maximalist movements.

## Design Rule

When a country file, focus tree, or event contradicts this document, the content must either:

- update this document,
- define an explicit alternate-start variant,
- or explain the contradiction in a comment or design note.
