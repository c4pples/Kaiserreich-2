# Factions and Ideological Blocs

Kaiserreich 2 should distinguish formal alliances from ideological families. A country can be ideologically sympathetic to a bloc without being militarily aligned to it.

## Formal Blocs

### Reichspakt

Leader: German Empire

Strategic purpose:

- Defend the post-Second Weltkrieg European settlement.
- Preserve Mitteleuropa as Germany's economic and logistical backbone.
- Prevent Russian revisionism and syndicalist restoration.
- Keep restored western states from drifting into hostile independence.

Internal tensions:

- Germany needs allies but often treats them as managed dependencies.
- Eastern European members want protection without permanent subordination.
- Austria-Hungary, if intact or reformed, cooperates with Berlin while resisting absorption into German strategy.
- Restored France and Britain may cooperate militarily while resenting German dominance.

### Entente

Core members: United Kingdom, French Republic, Canada, loyalist imperial governments, and surviving dominion structures.

Strategic purpose:

- Preserve restored legitimate governments.
- Rebuild naval, colonial, and financial networks.
- Prevent syndicalist return.
- Maintain enough autonomy that liberation does not become dependency on Berlin.

Internal tensions:

- Canada and the restored metropoles disagree over leadership.
- France wants continental freedom; Britain prioritizes sea lanes and imperial recovery.
- Colonial subjects question why liberation in Europe should mean restoration of empire overseas.
- Some Entente leaders prefer German partnership, while others see Germany as the next constraint to break.

### Moscow Accord

Leader: Russia

Strategic purpose:

- Overturn the German-dominated eastern settlement.
- Present Russia as protector of states constrained by Reichspakt security architecture.
- Build a land-power coalition capable of forcing Germany into a two-front crisis.
- Exploit anti-colonial, anti-German, and nationalist grievances.

Internal tensions:

- Russia's partners fear liberation becoming domination.
- Ideological identity varies by Russian path, so the bloc should be framed by revisionism first.
- Border states may accept Russian arms without accepting Russian leadership.
- Cooperation with Japan or syndicalist remnants is pragmatic, unstable, and deniable.

### Co-Prosperity Sphere

Leader: Japan

Strategic purpose:

- Secure Japanese primacy in East Asia and the western Pacific.
- Keep European empires from returning to prewar strength.
- Control resource flows, sea lanes, and client economies.
- Exploit any Atlantic crisis to expand strategic depth.

Internal tensions:

- Japan presents itself as anti-colonial while practicing imperial control.
- Client elites depend on Tokyo but fear permanent subordination.
- Conflict with Russia remains possible even when both oppose Germany or the Entente.
- The United States, if recovered, is the long-term Pacific counterweight.

## Ideological Families

### Conservative Restorationism

Common in restored Entente states and anti-syndicalist governments.

Traits:

- Legitimacy through continuity, monarchy, republican legality, or anti-revolutionary sacrifice.
- Suspicion of mass politics associated with the syndicalist period.
- Preference for security services, veterans' leagues, and disciplined reconstruction.
- Internal split between reformist reconciliation and punitive reaction.

### German-Liberal Authoritarian Constitutionalism

Common in the German sphere.

Traits:

- Constitutional language paired with executive security powers.
- Parliamentary politics constrained by military, imperial, and economic necessity.
- Technocratic reconstruction through Mitteleuropa institutions.
- Suspicion of both revolutionary socialism and uncontrolled nationalist revisionism.

### Revived Nationalism

The dominant radical pressure of the Third Weltkrieg era.

Traits:

- Defines politics through national recovery, sovereignty, grievance, and mobilization.
- Flourishes in states that were liberated, occupied, protected, partitioned, restored, or denied full equality after the Second Weltkrieg.
- Can be anti-German, anti-Russian, anti-Entente, anti-Japanese, anti-syndicalist, or anti-colonial depending on local context.
- Often expresses itself as National Populism when it becomes a mass ruling ideology, but can also pressure conservative, military, or social democratic governments.
- Turns faction systems brittle because allies want protection while rejecting subordination.

### Revolutionary Syndicalism

Survives underground after the defeat of the Internationale.

Traits:

- Underground unions, exile committees, labor networks, clandestine presses, and veteran cadres.
- Belief in worker self-management, industrial democracy, and mass labor organization.
- Capable of strikes, sabotage, agitation, and insurgent administration, but not automatically capable of state restoration.
- Often exploited by rival states despite mutual distrust.

### Totalism

Survives as the authoritarian revolutionary answer to defeat.

Traits:

- Argues that the Internationale failed because it lacked discipline, central command, and ideological ruthlessness.
- Builds cadre parties, security organs, militarized youth groups, and exile intelligence networks.
- Can cooperate with syndicalists tactically while preparing to dominate them.
- Attractive to defeated officers, radicalized veterans, and revolutionaries who see compromise as betrayal.

### Anarchism

Split from syndicalism as its own ideology.

Traits:

- Rejects state socialism, restored monarchism, parliamentary liberalism, and totalist party rule.
- Strongest in local communes, militias, dockworker networks, peasant self-defense movements, and anti-colonial guerrilla zones.
- Prefers federation, direct action, local autonomy, and anti-militarist rhetoric, though wartime necessity can create contradictions.
- Vulnerable to repression and infiltration because it resists centralized security structures.

### Russian Revisionism

Can be monarchist, republican, nationalist, or authoritarian depending on path.

Traits:

- Defines legitimacy through recovery from humiliation.
- Treats the German eastern order as artificial and temporary.
- Uses pan-Slavic, Orthodox, national, or anti-hegemonic language as appropriate to government path.
- Accepts ideological flexibility when the strategic target is Germany.
- Radical wings argue that Germany's second victory proves normal diplomacy is dead and that Russia must become a mobilized civilization-state.

### Anti-Colonial Nationalism

Rises across Africa, Asia, and the Middle East.

Traits:

- Learns from both syndicalist organization and nationalist statecraft.
- Sees restored European empires as weakened but dangerous.
- May accept Japanese, Russian, German, American, or syndicalist aid without permanent loyalty.
- Should not be written as a single ideology; local social coalitions matter.
- Frequently develops National Populist wings when veterans, officers, clerics, or charismatic parties fuse independence with authoritarian national renewal.

### American Reconstructionism

Depends on the American Civil War outcome.

Traits:

- Domestic legitimacy is tied to reunification, constitutional settlement, and economic reconstruction.
- Foreign policy debates are intense because overseas commitments compete with rebuilding.
- Any American bloc alignment should feel earned through crisis, trade, and ideology.
- The United States should be powerful but not frictionless.

## Bloc Design Rules

- Do not make all anti-German countries natural friends.
- Do not make all anti-syndicalist countries German clients.
- Do not treat the Entente as restored to 1914 confidence.
- Do not let syndicalist remnants function like a normal great-power faction unless a specific alternate-start path enables it.
- Do not make National Populism automatically pro-Axis, pro-German, or pro-Russian; in this setting it is a method of national revolt, not a single foreign alignment.
- Give middle powers reasons to bargain, defect, and hedge.
