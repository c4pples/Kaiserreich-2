# Third Weltkrieg Outbreak

The Third Weltkrieg should begin as a collapse of the postwar settlement, not as a clean great-power declaration.

Default outbreak model: the Client Revolts.

The Postwar Reckoning weakens Germany's financial order. Germany responds by demanding emergency compliance from Reichspakt clients, treaty states, and reconstruction-dependent partners. Several governments refuse, split, or fall to nationalist movements. Russia, France, Britain, Japan, and the American successor states then decide whether to intervene, mediate, exploit, or side with Germany.

## Core Thesis

Germany's Second Weltkrieg victory preserved control, but that control became too expensive and too intrusive. The Third Weltkrieg begins when Germany's clients decide that the price of protection is national subordination.

The war should feel like a loyalty crisis spreading into a world war.

## Suggested Outbreak Name

Possible in-universe names:

- The Client Revolts
- The Eastern Revolts
- The Mitteleuropa Crisis
- The Treaty Revolt
- The Sovereignty Crisis
- The Third Weltkrieg

Recommendation: use `The Mitteleuropa Crisis` for the opening event chain and `The Third Weltkrieg` once major powers formally enter.

## Escalation Chain

### Phase 1: Postwar Reckoning

The economic crisis hits. Germany and its banks try to preserve Mitteleuropa through emergency controls.

Events:

- reconstruction credit freezes,
- food and fuel prices rise,
- German inspectors demand budget oversight,
- veterans and nationalist parties organize,
- syndicalist and anarchist undergrounds exploit urban unrest,
- Russia and Japan begin covert funding.

### Phase 2: Emergency Compliance Demands

Berlin demands that clients accept a package of emergency measures.

Possible demands:

- debt restructuring under German supervision,
- railway and port priority for German military logistics,
- currency stabilization through Mitteleuropa clearing,
- anti-radical policing missions,
- officer exchanges or command integration,
- export quotas for food, coal, oil, or grain,
- restrictions on independent foreign policy.

Gameplay:

- each client receives a choice between compliance, negotiation, defiance, or internal crisis.
- Germany can offer concessions, threaten sanctions, deploy troops, or back loyalist factions.

### Phase 3: First Revolt

One major client refuses and becomes the spark.

Best candidates:

- Ukraine: food, grain, army loyalty, Russian agitation, and German extraction.
- Poland: sovereignty, army control, and anti-German nationalism.
- White Ruthenia/Belarus: exposed frontier, Russian pressure, and weak legitimacy.
- Baltic Federation/United Baltic Duchy: ethnic tension and German-Baltic privilege.

Recommended default spark: Ukraine.

Ukraine is large enough to matter, economically central to Mitteleuropa, close enough to Russia for intervention, and politically plausible as a place where German extraction, land politics, army nationalism, and Russian covert action collide.

### Phase 4: Revolt Wave

The first revolt forces every German-aligned frontier state to choose.

Possible outcomes:

- loyalist government remains with Germany,
- nationalist government defects,
- civil conflict between loyalists and nationalists,
- military junta declares armed neutrality,
- socialist or anarchist uprising opens a second internal front,
- Russia recognizes the rebel government,
- Germany directly intervenes.

Important design rule:

Not every puppet should revolt. Some should side with Germany because they fear Russia, fear domestic revolution, rely on German trade, or want German help against neighbors.

### Phase 5: Great-Power Decisions

The crisis becomes global through intervention decisions.

#### Germany

Choices:

- negotiate autonomy to preserve the bloc,
- impose military control,
- punish the first rebel to deter the rest,
- blame Russian sabotage and mobilize,
- sacrifice one client to save the wider system.

#### Russia

Choices:

- covertly arm rebels,
- recognize nationalist governments,
- demand German withdrawal,
- mobilize on the frontier,
- launch a limited intervention,
- escalate to general war.

#### France

Choices:

- support Germany to preserve anti-Russian security,
- stay neutral and rebuild,
- use the crisis to break treaty restrictions,
- back anti-German clients diplomatically,
- demand Alsace-Lorraine talks in exchange for cooperation.

#### United Kingdom

Choices:

- support continental stability,
- mediate through the Entente,
- prioritize imperial and naval recovery,
- exploit Germany's distraction to regain autonomy,
- fund anti-syndicalist but anti-German nationalist governments.

#### Japan

Choices:

- attack European concessions,
- pressure German East Asia,
- back Asian nationalist partners,
- expand resource control,
- avoid open war while Europe burns.

#### American Successor States

Choices:

- stay inward,
- seek recognition from Germany or the Entente,
- sell arms and food,
- send volunteers,
- back ideological partners,
- use the crisis to restart American reunification.

## Why Some States Side With Germany

The outbreak should not be a simple all-puppets-versus-Germany scenario.

States may remain loyal because:

- Russia is a more immediate threat than Germany,
- German guarantees protect them from neighbors,
- their ruling elites depend on German loans,
- their armies are integrated with German command,
- nationalist opposition is weaker than socialist opposition,
- Germany offers autonomy concessions,
- or local rivals join the anti-German side.

This creates replayability. Germany should be endangered, not doomed.

## Possible Starting Alignment Patterns

### Pattern A: Eastern Fire

Ukraine revolts. Poland hesitates. White Ruthenia stays loyal. Russia intervenes. Germany mobilizes. France and Britain debate intervention.

Best for default 1950 start.

### Pattern B: Baltic Spark

Baltic revolt creates a German-Russian naval and land crisis. Finland and Sweden become important. Britain is drawn by Baltic access.

Best for a northern-war variant.

### Pattern C: Polish Sovereignty Crisis

Poland rejects command integration. Germany threatens intervention. Russia recognizes Polish neutrality or backs nationalist factions. Austria-Hungary fears a wider Slavic revolt.

Best for a diplomatic crisis-heavy opening.

### Pattern D: Colonial Cascade

The first explosion happens in a German or Entente colonial mandate. Europe mobilizes after outside powers arm independence movements.

Best for an alternate global-south opening.

## Gameplay Requirements

- Client revolts should be partly predictable but not identical every run.
- Germany must have tools to keep some allies loyal.
- Russia must have tools to exploit the crisis short of immediate world war.
- France and Britain must face real strategic ambiguity.
- Japan should be able to globalize the crisis from Asia.
- American successor states should matter without needing a unified USA.
- Syndicalist, anarchist, and totalist movements should exploit chaos but not replace revived nationalism as the main trigger.

## Design Rule

The outbreak should begin with the question: who has the right to command a protected nation?

The Third Weltkrieg begins when that question stops being diplomatic and becomes military.
