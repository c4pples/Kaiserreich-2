# United States Fragmentation

The default Kaiserreich 2 canon keeps the United States fragmented after the Second American Civil War. This is the strongest option for balance, replayability, and continuity with a world where Germany has won the Second Weltkrieg and no clean liberal superpower has emerged to stabilize the international system.

## Core Assumption

No American claimant has reunified the continent by the 1950 start.

The civil war ended through exhaustion, armistices, foreign pressure, economic collapse, and local security settlements rather than unconditional victory. The former United States is therefore a broken strategic space: too powerful to ignore, too divided to dominate the world immediately.

## Why Fragmentation Works

- It prevents the United States from deciding the Third Weltkrieg before the game begins.
- It gives Canada, Mexico, the Caribbean, Japan, Germany, and the Entente meaningful American policy.
- It lets the player choose reunification, permanent division, or ideological realignment.
- It preserves the trauma of the Second American Civil War as active history rather than background flavor.
- It creates a western mirror to Eastern Europe: borders exist, but everyone knows they are provisional.

## Recommended Successor Map

The exact state borders can be tuned for HOI4 gameplay, but the default political geography should be:

### Federal Republic

Likely territory:

- New England,
- the Mid-Atlantic,
- parts of the Upper Midwest,
- Washington, D.C. if it survives as a federal enclave or protected capital.

Character:

- Claims constitutional continuity.
- Exhausted, legalistic, and dependent on loans, Canadian access, and surviving federal institutions.
- Wants reunification but fears another total war.

Foreign posture:

- Courted by Canada and the Entente.
- Suspicious of German economic penetration.
- Hostile to revolutionary movements but not automatically willing to fight overseas.

### Combined Syndicates Remnant

Likely territory:

- Great Lakes industrial belt,
- Chicago or another major industrial capital,
- possibly parts of the northern Mississippi basin.

Character:

- The largest surviving revolutionary American state.
- Internally split between syndicalists, totalists, anarchists, and democratic labor factions.
- Becomes the most important refuge for defeated European revolutionaries if it survives.

Foreign posture:

- Supports underground syndicalists abroad.
- May receive covert Russian aid if anti-German priorities align.
- Deeply hostile to Canada, restored Britain, and anti-syndicalist American claimants.

### American Union State or National State

Likely territory:

- Lower South,
- parts of the interior,
- Gulf Coast access if not partitioned.

Character:

- Populist, nationalist, anti-syndicalist, and suspicious of old federal elites.
- Can evolve toward personalist rule, clerical populism, military nationalism, or a more conventional conservative republic.
- Claims to represent the betrayed American majority rather than old constitutional legality.

Foreign posture:

- Anti-syndicalist but not automatically pro-Entente.
- Suspicious of German finance, Canadian influence, Mexican claims, and Japanese Pacific moves.
- May become the most volatile reunification claimant.

### Pacific States

Likely territory:

- Pacific Coast,
- mountain approaches depending on ceasefire lines.

Character:

- Commercial, naval, technocratic, and survival-focused.
- More interested in Pacific security than eastern reunification at first.
- Balances democratic institutions with emergency military and intelligence powers.

Foreign posture:

- Watches Japan as the central threat.
- May accept German, Entente, or local Asian partnerships.
- Could become the bridge between American reunification and Pacific war.

### Neutral or Contested Interior

Likely territory:

- Great Plains,
- Rockies,
- borderland zones,
- demilitarized transport corridors.

Character:

- Not empty space, but a patchwork of state governments, military districts, native authorities, militias, company towns, and reconstruction zones.
- Useful for limited wars, border incidents, volunteers, and influence races.

Foreign posture:

- Courted by all American claimants.
- Vulnerable to smuggling, radical organizing, and foreign intelligence.

## Canon Status of Reunification

Reunification should be possible but not assumed.

Good paths:

- negotiated federal convention,
- military reunification,
- syndicalist continental revolution,
- nationalist march on Washington,
- Pacific-led constitutional reset,
- permanent American confederation,
- foreign-guaranteed partition.

Bad default:

- a fully restored United States already acting as a global superpower on day one.

## Gameplay Systems

- Add an American armistice system tracking ceasefire legitimacy.
- Use border incidents before full wars.
- Track foreign recognition separately from actual control.
- Let external powers support claimants through loans, advisers, volunteers, intelligence, and trade access.
- Make reunification a mid-game prize that changes the world balance.
