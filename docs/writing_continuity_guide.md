# Writing and Continuity Guide

Kaiserreich 2 should read like a continuation of Kaiserreich: grounded, political, historically literate, and attentive to national character. The tone should avoid parody, meme writing, and modern ideological shorthand.

## Voice

- Write events as political history seen from inside the country, not as omniscient lore dumps.
- Let governments justify themselves in their own language.
- Treat all factions as internally divided and historically conditioned.
- Prefer plausible institutional conflict over sudden personality-driven twists.
- Use irony sparingly and never at the expense of the setting's seriousness.

## Event Structure

Good event text should usually answer:

- What happened?
- Why does this matter now?
- Which institutions or factions are reacting?
- What does the player decision represent?

Avoid:

- explaining all background in one paragraph,
- reducing ideologies to slogans,
- writing every crisis as a world-ending moment,
- or making countries aware of "the timeline" as if they are outside history.

## Country Characterization

### Germany

Germany should sound confident but burdened. Its leaders believe they saved Europe twice, yet they know the system requires constant management. German content should emphasize law, order, planning, alliance discipline, and anxiety about encirclement.

### France

Restored France should sound wounded, proud, and politically sharp. It is not simply "old France back again." It carries exile memory, civil trauma, anti-syndicalist justice, and fear of dependency on Germany.

### Britain

Restored Britain should sound restrained and institutional, but not serene. Its political class must reconcile monarchy, labor, empire, and social compromise after revolution and return.

### Russia

Russia should sound expansive, aggrieved, and historic. Its exact ideological vocabulary depends on government path, but the strategic story is recovery from humiliation and rejection of German ordering.

### Japan

Japan should sound modernizing, disciplined, and strategically patient. Its rhetoric of Asian liberation should always sit beside the reality of hierarchy and coercion.

### United States

American writing should be shaped by reconstruction. Whether democratic, authoritarian, socialist, or fractured, the country should be written as a giant recovering from internal catastrophe, not as an untouched arsenal.

## Ideology Handling

- Syndicalism after defeat should be written as memory, underground organization, exile, and labor grievance before it becomes state power again.
- Conservatism should not be flat reaction; distinguish constitutional monarchists, military authoritarians, clericals, liberals, and technocrats.
- National populism should emerge from humiliation, paramilitary culture, ethnic politics, or imperial crisis, not as generic villainy.
- Market liberal and social liberal content should address reconstruction, debt, labor peace, and constitutional legitimacy.
- Radical socialism should vary by country: labor federation, agrarian revolution, anti-colonial liberation, or clandestine resistance.

## Continuity Checklist

Before adding content, confirm:

- Which Second Weltkrieg outcome does this file assume?
- Which government path is active for each major affected country?
- Does this event require a faction member, ideological sympathy, or only diplomatic contact?
- Does the country speak in a way consistent with its KR characterization?
- Does the outcome create future hooks rather than closing the story completely?
- Could this crisis support multiple player responses?

## Modularity Rules

- Put global assumptions in shared documentation or scripted flags.
- Keep country-specific alternate outcomes localized to that country unless they must affect a global system.
- Use start-game rules for major uncertain outcomes such as the American Civil War, Russian government, and Danubian settlement.
- Avoid writing one country's content that silently assumes another country's most extreme path.
- Add small design notes near complex focus branches explaining their assumed global context.
